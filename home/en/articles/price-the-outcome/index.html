Price the outcome, not the hour

Rima Yaqub, November 2026, 5 min read

The same service sells for 100 with one provider and 1,000 with another. In most cases the difference isn’t skill. It’s what each of them is actually selling.

The first sells time: an hour, a session, a treatment. The second sells an outcome: a clear change whose value the client understands before paying.

Why hourly pricing becomes a ceiling

When price is tied to the hour, income is capped by the hours in a day, and every attempt to grow means more work, not more profit. Worse, clients compare your hour with someone else’s, and the decision turns into a price comparison.

A simple pricing formula

Price is built from three elements: the value of the outcome to the client, the risk it helps them avoid, and the urgency they feel. Ask: how much does the client save? What risk do they avoid? How much pain goes away?

The service is then built as a package tied to that outcome, with a clear path from start to finish, instead of a list of separate sessions.

Raising prices starts inside

Many owners fear raising prices because they justify them with effort. Once price rests on clear value, the justifying stops, and the conversation is about the outcome, not the number.

Don’t ask how many hours your service is worth. Ask what the change you create is worth.
