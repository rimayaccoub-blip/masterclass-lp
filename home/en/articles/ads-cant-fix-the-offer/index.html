Advertising can’t fix an offer that isn’t ready

Rima Yaqub, November 2026, 7 min read

“I tried ads, and they didn’t work.” I hear it often, and it’s usually true. But when we open up what actually happened, the problem is rarely the ad itself.

Advertising is an amplifier. It takes what already exists and puts it in front of more people. If the offer is clear and the price is built on value, it amplifies the result. If not, it amplifies the problem and burns money faster.

Why we jump to advertising

Because it looks like the fastest fix. When the calendar is empty, the first question is: how much do I pay to get clients? And ads create a sense of movement: numbers appear, messages arrive, something happens.

But advertising answers one question only: how people reach you. The others, what you offer, at what price, and what happens after a client writes to you, stay exactly as they were. If they’re unanswered, the ad simply brings people to a door that isn’t ready.

Where the client gets lost between the ad and the decision

I always think of the path as a chain: ad, message, conversation or booking, meeting, decision. Every link can break. Most owners measure only the first one: how much did the lead cost?

An example from business

Quibi: $1.75 billion, six months

The video platform Quibi raised about $1.75 billion from investors, spent generously on marketing, and launched in April 2020 with a large campaign and famous names. About six months later it announced it was shutting down.

Visibility wasn’t the problem. People knew it existed. The problem was that the offer itself wasn’t clear enough to pay for: why subscribe to this service in particular? When that answer is missing, every dollar of advertising simply carries an unconvincing message to more people.

Three signs the offer isn’t ready yet

The service is described as a number of sessions. “Five sessions for this price” tells the client nothing about what will change in their life or business. Clients don’t buy sessions; they buy outcomes.

Every inquiry turns into a price question. When value isn’t clear, price becomes the only thing left to compare.

There is no path after the click. The ad brings a message, then nothing clear: no structured reply, no diagnosis, no next step, no follow-up.

The right order

In the RIMA Method, attracting clients is stage six, not stage one. Before it come diagnosis, positioning, packages, pricing and message. That doesn’t mean postponing marketing for months. It means making sure every link in the path is ready before you pour money into it.

Once the links are ready, advertising becomes a measurable investment: you know what a lead costs, how many book, how many show up and how many buy. And you know which link needs work.

Exercise: check the path before your next campaign

  1. Write down the path as it is today, from the first message to payment, step by step.
  2. For each step: what happens? Who is responsible? How long does it take?
  3. Count your last ten inquiries: how many booked? How many showed up? How many bought?
  4. Where is the biggest drop? That is the link to work on first, before any extra budget.
  5. Write one name, one price and one clear outcome for your main offer, in a single sentence.

Key takeaways

  • Advertising amplifies what exists, the strong and the weak alike.
  • “Ads didn’t work” usually means another link in the path broke.
  • Measure the whole path, not just the cost per lead.
  • A clear offer with one name, one price and structured follow-up comes before any budget.

Before asking how much to spend on ads, ask: is what I’m about to promote ready to be bought?

Sources: Coverage of Quibi’s shutdown, October 2020 (The Wall Street Journal, NBC News).
