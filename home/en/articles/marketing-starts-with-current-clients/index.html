Marketing starts with the client you already have

Rima Yaqub, November 2026, 8 min read

Most marketing budgets go to people who don’t know you yet. Very little goes to the person who already bought from you and was happy with the result. Yet that person is the shortest path to stable income.

The problem: a business that starts from zero every month

In many service businesses, the relationship ends with the last session. The client got a result, thanked you, and left. Then the new month starts with the same question: where will the next client come from?

So the whole plan is built around acquisition: an ad, a post, a new offer. It’s like filling a leaking bucket. The more you pour in, the more leaks out of the bottom.

Do you know how many clients came back to you this year? How many didn’t, and why? If the answer isn’t clear, this is where the work begins.

What the numbers say

Research by Frederick Reichheld at Bain & Company, summarised in his book The Loyalty Effect, showed that a small improvement in client retention can change profit dramatically.

5% → 25–95%According to Bain research, a 5% increase in client retention was linked to a 25% to 95% increase in profits, depending on the industry.

The reason is that an existing client doesn’t need an ad to find you or persuasion to trust you. They tend to buy more over time, and they bring others. All of it without a new acquisition cost.

From practice

A fitness club attracting members and losing them just as quietly

Working with a fitness club, the first complaint was “we need more members”. But when we built the income and expense tables, the real picture emerged: a high share of members were leaving after a short time, and there was no distinction between two very different audiences.

Instead of a bigger acquisition campaign, we started from the inside: we defined two audiences with different pricing for each, sent members a feedback survey, built communication with current and former members, and added a simple service element that could actually be delivered, a monthly progress measurement. Campaigns came afterwards, along with a system that keeps the people who join.

Four steps to a system that keeps clients

1. A path after the service. What happens after the last session? A follow-up message two weeks later, a question about the result, an invitation to the next step. If the answer is “nothing”, income is leaking.

2. A continuation offer. A follow-up package, a maintenance session, or a monthly plan for clients who got a result and want to keep it. This is the recurring income that stops each month from starting at zero.

3. Structured listening. A short survey, a call, or one question at the end of the service: what mattered most to you? The answers become your next marketing message, in the client’s own words.

4. A referral invitation. A satisfied client is the strongest marketing channel, but rarely refers on their own. They need a clear invitation, at the right moment: when they see the result.

Three common mistakes

Waiting for the client to come back on their own. Clients are busy with their lives. If you don’t invite them to the next step, they won’t remember it.

Offering old clients a discount just to return. A satisfied client doesn’t need a discount. They need a clear reason to continue, tied to a result they want to keep.

Not measuring return. If you don’t know the share of clients who come back, you won’t know whether what you’re doing works. One simple number in your financial file is enough to start.

And advertising?

Attracting new clients is essential for any growing business, and I build campaigns within my engagements. But order matters. When a system keeps clients after the purchase, every new client becomes more valuable, and every unit spent on ads returns more. Without it, advertising simply fills the leaking bucket faster.

Exercise: map your current clients

  1. List everyone who bought from you in the last 12 months.
  2. Next to each name: did they come back? Did they refer anyone? When was the last contact?
  3. Choose five clients who got a good result and contact them this week with one question: how are you doing today with what we built?
  4. Write one simple continuation offer that suits them.
  5. A month later, compare: how much income came from this step, without any ads?

Key takeaways

  • The client you already have is the shortest path to stable income.
  • According to Bain, a small improvement in retention can change profit dramatically.
  • Every business needs a post-service path, a continuation offer and a referral invitation.
  • Advertising works better when clients enter a system that keeps them.

Before your next campaign, ask: what do I offer the people who have already bought from me?

Sources: Frederick F. Reichheld, The Loyalty Effect, Harvard Business School Press, 1996. Bain & Company.
