Pricing: the profit lever few owners pull

Rima Yaqub, November 2026, 5 min read

When owners want more profit, they think about more clients first. They rarely think about price, even though the numbers say price is the strongest lever.

What the research says

In a study published in Harvard Business Review in 1992, McKinsey’s Michael Marn and Robert Rosiello analysed data from more than two thousand companies. They found that a price improvement of just 1%, with volume unchanged, raised operating profit by about 11% on average. That is a far larger effect than cutting costs or growing volume by the same percentage.

The reason is simple: every unit added to the price goes straight to profit, because the cost hasn’t changed.

That is why Warren Buffett has said the single most important decision in evaluating a business is its pricing power: can it raise prices without losing its clients?

Why we avoid raising prices

In service businesses, price feels personal. The owner feels they are pricing themselves, not their service. So they justify, apologise and offer a discount before anyone asks.

The result is a price built on fear and comparison, not on the value of the outcome.

Where to begin

Start with two questions: what is the outcome worth to the client? And what is your real profit from each service today? Once both answers are clear, raising a price becomes a considered decision, not a gamble.

Before you look for a new client, review the price of the clients you already have.

Sources: Michael V. Marn & Robert L. Rosiello, “Managing Price, Gaining Profit”, Harvard Business Review, 1992. Warren Buffett, interview with the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission (FCIC), 2010.
