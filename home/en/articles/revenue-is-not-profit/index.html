Revenue is not profit

Rima Yaqub, November 2026, 4 min read

The sentence I hear most from service business owners is: “Money comes in, but I don’t feel it.” It isn’t an exaggeration. In many businesses, revenue grows year after year while profit stays exactly where it was.

The cause is rarely a lack of clients. The cause is that the owner looks at a single number: how much came in this month. The more important question, how much is left and where it came from, goes unanswered.

Three places where profit disappears

Services that look profitable and aren’t. The service in highest demand can be the least profitable once real time, materials and preparation are counted. High demand is not high margin.

Time that never made it into the price. The one-hour session has quietly become three hours of follow-ups, messages and revisions, while the price stayed the same.

Small costs that add up. Subscriptions, tools and “one-time” discounts. Each is small; together they consume an entire margin.

Where to begin

Start with a simple financial file for each service: price, actual time, direct cost and monthly volume. Within an hour you will see which service carries the business and which one drains it.

From there, decisions become clear: which price to raise, which service to rebuild as a package, and which to stop selling.

Real growth doesn’t start with a new ad. It starts with knowing precisely what stays in your hands.
